Quantity grammar

Two packaging units and one measure. Than — the atomic roll, one inventory row, carrying its own yardage. Bale — a container of thans under one printed bale number; the roster count varies, and one bale may hold several shades. Yards — the continuous measure.
Every line speaks the item's own packaging: a movement that took the whole roster counts in bales, anything less counts in thans. 6B · 250t · 4B + 21t. Yards print alongside.
Never both units for the same goods. "2B · 4 thans" restates one delivery twice — banned. A bale's than count and yardage come only from its own roster, never from a conversion factor.
Gap arithmetic is settled in yards — the only universally additive number across mixed bale and than records. Packaging shows beside it for recognition, never as the subtraction.
